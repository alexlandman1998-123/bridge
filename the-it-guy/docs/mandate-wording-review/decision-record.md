# Mandate wording review and decision record

Prepared on 4 October 2026 for the Arch9 primary transaction workspace. This is the Phase 1 deliverable for the mandate wording and presentation plan agreed in this conversation. It contains three complete proposed contracts and the decisions needed to prepare them for legal review. It does not approve, publish or activate any wording.

The confirmed product decision is that sole and exclusive are the same mandate type and the generated title remains **Exclusive mandate to sell**. The other commercial rules below are recommendations expressed in the drafts. Preparing those drafts is authorised; their status is not a claim that the requester or counsel has approved every proposed term.

## Complete drafts

| Mandate | Draft | Commercial model proposed for review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exclusive including sole | [Exclusive mandate draft](exclusive-mandate-draft.md) | One agency for a defined period. A qualifying sale during the effective exclusive period attracts commission even if the Seller or another person finds the buyer, subject to identified exclusions and lawful entitlement. |
| Open | [Open mandate draft](open-mandate-draft.md) | Other agencies and independent private sales are permitted. Commission requires effective cause. The default proposed appointment is indefinite and the Seller can cancel on receipt of recorded notice. |
| Dual | [Dual mandate draft](dual-mandate-draft.md) | One shared appointment with two named agencies, both accepting the same terms. No third agency during the restricted period without agreement. Commission requires effective cause, with one combined seller fee and an express allocation rule. Independent private sales do not earn agency commission without effective cause. |

Each draft includes its own parties, property and terms schedule, seven substantive clauses, marketing commitments, expenses, disclosure and authority requirements, Special Conditions, seller signatures and agency acceptance. Dual has a separate acceptance for each agency. The bracketed entries are required capture slots, not supplied facts or approved merge fields.

## Legal review required before activation

Counsel must review the exact three drafts and their completed agency-specific schedules. Approval of an older template must not be carried over automatically. The existing [vNext approval evidence](../../config/mandate-template-vnext-approval-evidence.json) relates to its own earlier content; these contracts require their own approval evidence and version identifiers.

The following questions must be resolved before these drafts become signing templates:

1. Confirm the Exclusive private-sale commission trigger, its exclusions and its fairness and enforceability in the intended agency and consumer circumstances. Check the required written explanation of private sales and specific marketing obligations. The terminology alone does not establish a statutory private-sale fee.
2. Resolve the interaction of the sole-mandate prohibition on tacit renewal or continuation, the proposed express end-date direction and any applicable fixed-term consumer continuation rule. Confirm the validity and timing of the Seller's end instruction and the expiry notice process. Do not activate the drafts with this interaction unresolved.
3. Determine statutory applicability for the actual parties and transaction, rather than assuming that every company or trust follows the same rules. Confirm the proposed contractual 20-business-day right and the lawful assessment of cancellation charges. No clause may waive a mandatory cancellation or cooling-off right.
4. Approve an agency-specific cooling-off notice for the actual acquisition and signing route. Review direct marketing, electronic consumer transactions, exclusions and any early service-start request. The drafts preserve applicable rights; Phase 1 does not assert one cooling-off period for every route.
5. Confirm the combined Dual fee, allocation, differing VAT registrations, invoicing and dispute arrangements. Both agencies must expressly accept those terms. The fee limit cannot extinguish claims under outside agreements.
6. Confirm the deliberate protection-window and buyer-list conditions, excluded buyers, and how accrued rights, failed suspensive conditions, failed transfer and alleged breach or loss are treated. The proposed window must not become an implied indefinite claim.
7. Confirm owner and representative authority requirements, including applicable spouse and trustee consent, the FFC and registration requirements for the business and relevant persons, and disclosure before acceptance. Printing a certificate number is not verification.
8. Confirm the lawful electronic-signature route for this mandate and the access to complete signing evidence. Do not assume that ordinary electronic signing is sufficient for every legally required signature or for a property sale agreement.
9. Approve each agency's privacy notice, lawful processing and sharing basis, retention, Information Officer details and PAIA information. Signing must not be treated as blanket consent to promotional marketing or unrestricted sharing of seller identity documents.

## Sources and limits of the review

The drafts are original proposed contractual wording. The periods, service commitments, expense rules and fee choices in the decision table are product proposals. They are not prescribed contract text or a certification of legal compliance. Source review supports the issues to send to counsel, whose review must also confirm the current applicable law and agency circumstances.

| Primary source | Issue supported by the source |
| --- | --- |
| [Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201910/42746gon1295.pdf), sections 56 and 67 | Remuneration is subject to certificate requirements. A practitioner must receive the completed, signed prescribed disclosure before accepting a mandate. The disclosure must be provided and attached as required in the sale process. |
| [Property Practitioners Regulations 2022](https://www.saflii.org/za/gaz/ZAGovGaz/2022/26.pdf), regulation 34 | Written sole-mandate terms, renewal restrictions, and written explanations of private-sale implications and specific marketing obligations. The indexed Gazette extracts were available; direct access to the full Gazette was unavailable during preparation. Counsel must check the complete current regulations. |
| [Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/321864670.pdf), sections 14 and 16 | Fixed-term cancellation and expiry processes where applicable, and conditional direct-marketing cooling-off rights. Section 14 excludes transactions between juristic persons; the proposed contractual right is broader. |
| [Consumer Protection Regulations](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/34180rg9515gon293.pdf), regulation 5 | Cancellation-charge reasonableness is assessed in the circumstances and must not negate the cancellation right. |
| [Electronic Communications and Transactions Act 25 of 2002](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a25-02.pdf), sections 13 and 42 to 44 | Electronic-signature requirements and conditional electronic-consumer rights must be assessed for the particular route. A mandate and a land sale are different documents. |
| [Wakefields Real Estate v Attree](https://www3.saflii.org/za/cases/ZASCA/2011/161.html), Supreme Court of Appeal, 28 September 2011 | An effective-cause dispute resulted in additional commission liability despite payment to another agency. Naming agencies or asserting one fee cannot bind an outside agency. The judgment was reviewed through the available primary court text in the preceding wording review. |

## Capture requirements for Phase 2

Phase 2 should use the existing seller, property, corrections and snapshot patterns. It should preserve historic aliases rather than rewriting existing contracts. New entries must distinguish **not supplied**, **not applicable** and an express **None**; a blank must not silently approve an exclusion or a zero commission.

Capture needs include full contracting agency identities and certificate evidence, each agency's authorised acceptance, seller capacity and authority references, valid period instructions, explicit fee and VAT calculations, named exclusions and existing introductions, marketing tasks and expense approvals, agreed protection days, notice contacts, and identified annexures. Dual additionally needs both agency notice contacts, a complete allocation rule and access restricted to the agreed mandate information.

The Open draft deliberately permits an indefinite appointment. The current correction validator requires an end date for every type, so Phase 2 must reconcile that requirement before using the new Open draft. The current Dual renderer captures only a second agency name and prints one agency acceptance; that is insufficient for the proposed shared contract.

Commercial defaults must be visible, editable and expressly accepted in the completed terms. Draft recommendations must not silently alter an existing appointment. All required data and the approved wording must be frozen together in a reviewed version in the later signing-integration phase.

## Clean design requirements for Phase 3

Use one restrained A4 design for all three variants. Present the primary parties, Property, effective period, commission and VAT in a clear first-page summary. Put detailed authority, marketing, exclusions and allocation schedules in logically ordered sections. Use white space, consistent alignment, minimal borders and limited brand-colour accents, with a proportioned logo and readable contrast.

Use readable body text around 10.5 to 11 pt. Private-sale liability and marketing commitments must have the same readable prominence as the main terms, not smaller footnotes. Use numbered clauses and content-aware pagination, keep headings with their text and signature blocks together, and avoid large empty areas, clipped text or blank pages.

Keep all required seller signatures and, for Dual, both agency acceptances spacious and clearly labelled. Footer information should include reference, approved wording version and page numbering. Do not print internal workflow identifiers as customer headings.

The earlier plan's three-to-four-page target is a design aspiration for ordinary inputs, not a hard limit. These are full review drafts with schedules; legal review and layout work should shorten repetition where safe. Long conditions, additional owners and both Dual agency details may require extra pages. Do not omit terms or shrink text merely to meet a page count. PDF layout has not been implemented or visually certified in Phase 1.
